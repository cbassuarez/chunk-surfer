# The phone box, 1988

Decided with Seb, 2026-09-13. The phone box on the arrival road is rebuilt
around one idea: it should hurt. Everything below was chosen, not assumed.

Updated with Seb, 2026-10-21: the rotary remains fully usable, but progression
never requires dialling or tape payment. The key and hold-music sheet calls ring
the box when available. Lifting the receiver plays their authored lines; the
loop pass also releases on pickup, without requiring speech or a dialogue choice.
Outgoing calls retain the mechanics and costs below.

The booth is the existing three-dimensional K6, viewed from inside. Right-drag
or the arrow keys turn the head. The cabinet's live controls and the directory
on the shelf share the world dither; the number clues occupy the book margin.
The final recording holds movement only for its playback, including after a
pause or reload, and releases it on completion.

## The set

- **The network is 1988 GPO/BT, and only the phone box is.** The rest of the game
  keeps whatever era it has; out here the network is from before, and that is
  part of what is wrong.
- **A grey pay-on-answer coinbox with a black rotary dial.** No display (those
  boxes had none).
- **Dialled by pointer only.** Drag a finger hole round to the finger stop and let
  go; the dial returns at its own speed and every pulse clicks down the line.
  No keyboard digits, no undo: a wrong digit means pressing the cradle and
  starting again. No Enter: the exchange connects on its own once the number is
  complete, or after a pause.
- **Pay on answer.** Dialling is free. When the far end answers he hears them
  through rapid pips, and they hear only pips, until money goes in. Made legible
  the first time by the box itself: the period instruction plate (WHEN YOU HEAR
  RAPID PIPS INSERT MONEY), the slot lit while the pips sound, and one thought.
- **Coins only, and he has none. The tape fits.** The DA-1000's tape goes into the
  coin slot. The swallowed tape **records the call over his takes**, visible
  through a small window in the coinbox where it turns, room labels sliding past.
  **The loss is permanent for the run**: a take recorded over is gone, and the
  room no longer counts. Out here there is no going back to record it again.
- Free, as on a real box: 999, 100, 123, 192.

## Finding numbers (the "remember" menu is gone)

- **His van's car phone**: on a Dymo label on the van's dash. Read it outside.
- **Lyle's**: he still knows it by heart and says it the first time he lifts the
  receiver. It is the payphone in his undergraduate hall in Leeds.
- **Sarah's**: 192 refuses her (ex-directory). The chained directory's page with
  her surname is torn out. The page turns up on the road.
- **Her sister's**: listed in the chained directory, where Sarah's is missing.
- **Worked into the book margin**: other people's numbers, and a redundant set with
  missing digits that point to the van and to what is outside.
- **Lodge and office**: the lodge on the card; the office in its old form.

## Who answers

- **Lyle** (an old flame from undergrad — another Sarah to him, another thing he
  let slip): the hall payphone. A student answers over a party. Nobody called
  Lyle lives there.
- **Sarah**: someone else answers. "Hello? ... Sarah, it's no one." If he gave her
  name at the gate, the man knows who it is: "She's gone up." Costs one
  composure grid, once.
- **His flat**: his own answerphone. Whatever he leaves there, with the mic on and
  consent given, is kept and plays back from the tape (the old voicemail,
  moved).
- **Her sister**: will not pick up for him.
- **999, 100, 123, 192, lodge, office**: as the network does. The key call (from the
  cold-open trunk) and the hold-music sheet stay, once ever, retold for 1988.

## Calling himself, by pass

The phone box now repeats with the scene: the same box on every pass, worn and
broken with the rest.

- **Early passes (1–9)**: dial his car phone and it rings in the van up the road.
  He can leave the receiver hanging and go and answer it, and hear the box: rain
  on the glass, the receiver swinging.
- **Pass 14**: the box rings as he reaches it, and the scene repeats — the same
  pass, again — until he **lifts the receiver**. On the line, at once: the van's car phone still off the hook
  from pass 1 (the cab, the rain on the windscreen, his own breathing) and
  somebody waiting, who says "there you are", and hangs up.
- **Late passes (22 on)**: lifting the receiver, he is already on the line with
  himself. His choice whether to lift it. With the mic on and consent given,
  what he says comes back delayed and fainter; without it, breathing and this
  same box, a few seconds behind. Costs one composure grid, once.

## Her page, and the flurry

- **It keeps finding him**: in a lodge ledger early, caught on the fence on a
  broken pass, on the van's dashboard late.
- **From pass 22 it is inescapable**: a flurry of pages round him, in the world
  and scrawled across the edges of the screen. Every page has her number and a
  message about how he let it all go, in **everyone's** words — his, hers,
  Lyle's, the guard's form. Pages close enough can be read. It **drains
  composure while it lasts**, never below the floor, and only stepping onto the
  tape stops it.
